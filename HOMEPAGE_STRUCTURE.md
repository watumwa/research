# Homepage Structure and Design Direction

This homepage is intentionally designed to feel **editorial, credible and human-made**, rather than like a generic AI-generated SaaS landing page.

## 1. Header

Keep navigation simple and useful:

- Products
- How it works
- Inside the platform
- Access
- About
- Contact
- Sign in
- Create account

Avoid decorative utility links unless they serve a real function.

## 2. Hero

### Message

**Build better research. Communicate with confidence.**

Supporting copy should immediately explain what the platform actually helps a learner do:

- define a research problem;
- structure Chapter One;
- review literature;
- improve professional communication;
- learn through guided lessons, real examples and builders.

### Visual treatment

Use a real/editorial learner photograph as the visual anchor rather than floating decorative UI cards. The current build includes `frontend/public/images/hero-research-learning.jpg` as the working hero visual.

For final production, the best replacement would be a genuine client-approved photograph of a learner, training session, classroom, founder or research workspace.

## 3. Audience strip

A restrained line naming the core audiences:

- University students
- Researchers
- Professionals
- Institutions

Do not use invented learner counts or ratings until the client can verify them.

## 4. Learning pathways

Present the two products as clear editorial rows rather than identical marketing cards.

### The Research Blueprint

Focus on:

- research-problem diagnosis;
- Chapter One;
- literature review;
- methodology;
- guided builders.

### Business Communication Toolkit

Focus on:

- professional writing;
- speaking with confidence;
- meetings;
- realistic workplace practice.

## 5. Explain → Model → Practice → Apply

This is the product's strongest learning idea and should remain the central method across the site and learner interface.

Each step should explain what the learner actually does, rather than being used only as a decorative four-card pattern.

## 6. Inside the platform

Show concrete evidence of the product rather than abstract marketing claims.

The homepage now previews:

1. **Problem Analysis — Premium**
   - Condition-based problems
   - Process-based problems
   - Historical/narrative problems
   - Comparative/disparity problems

2. **Chapter One Builder**
   - Background
   - Problem statement
   - Purpose/objectives
   - Research questions
   - Gap/significance

3. **Literature Review Method**
   - Understand
   - Extract
   - Compare
   - Synthesise

## 7. Who it is for

Use descriptive rows rather than generic icons:

- University students
- Researchers
- Professionals
- Institutions and teams

This makes the page feel more authored and less template-driven.

## 8. Access

Explain the business model without hiding it:

### Free foundation access
Selected lessons, free resources, account and progress tracking.

### Individual premium resources
Specialist materials such as Problem Analysis are unlocked through Django entitlements after payment verification.

### Full pathway access
Complete course access for learners who want the full sequence.

Prices should only be published after the client confirms them. Do not invent public pricing or testimonials.

## 9. Founder

Use the supplied founder information rather than a fake portrait or invented biography.

Current homepage includes:

A genuine founder photograph should be added when the client supplies one.

## 10. FAQ

Answer real objections:

- What is free?
- Is Problem Analysis paid?
- Do the builders write the research for the learner?
- Is learner work saved?
- Can institutions use the system?

## 11. Contact

Keep the real Django-backed contact form.

## 12. Final call to action

Use one simple promise:

**Start with one lesson. Build a skill you can reuse.**

# Design rules used to avoid an “AI-designed” appearance

- Avoid fake ratings, user counts and testimonials.
- Avoid excessive floating glass cards.
- Avoid decorative gradients everywhere.
- Use fewer rounded cards and more editorial borders/rows.
- Use real course language and real examples from the client material.
- Use asymmetry where useful instead of perfectly mirrored blocks.
- Keep typography restrained and readable.
- Let the actual product content provide visual interest.
- Prefer real photography over generic abstract illustrations.
- Keep CTA wording practical rather than exaggerated.

# Still requiring client-provided production content

The system is functional, but these items should come from the client before final launch:

- genuine founder photograph;
- genuine learner/training photographs if available;
- verified testimonials (do not fabricate them);
- final public pricing and subscription terms;
- privacy policy;
- terms of use;
- refund/cancellation policy for paid digital content;
- final contact email/phone/social links;
- live payment-provider credentials;
- production SMTP/email credentials;
- domain/hosting details.
